    Diagrams

    The architecture and installation diagrams of the documentation, drawn in the colours of the interface theme. dtaas-diagrams.drawio is the source, with one diagram per page, and the PNG files in the documentation are exported from it.

    The scripts that make and export the file are the update-docs-diagrams agent skill, in .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/. Its SKILL.md gives the steps, and every command below runs from the repository root.

    Light and Dark Variants

    Every page under docs/ is exported twice, except a page in LIGHT_ONLY. write_dark in design/dark.py turns each light fill, including the white drawio draws when a shape names none, into the slate page of the site with a fifth of its own colour, so tints keep their hue. Dark lines and dark stencil icons become light, text takes the light or the dark text colour by contrast with its new fill, and the DTaaS lockup and the GitLab logo become their white versions. The traefik and Docker logos have no white version, so they are drawn unchanged on a light plate, listed in DARK_LETTERING. An embedded SVG whose every colour falls below 3 to 1 on slate, such as the navy wind turbines, is redrawn in the light line colour. Text drawn in the colour of its ground stays hidden, as it is in the light image. The pages show the pair with #only-light and #only-dark, and the PDF prints the light one.

    The export stops when a cell of a placed page has text below 4.5 to 1 or a line or border below 3 to 1 in the dark variant, unless the cell is listed in CONTRAST_EXCEPTIONS in config/pages.py with its reason. The list is empty: every dark image meets both minimums.

    A page listed in LIGHT_ONLY in config/pages.py gets no dark variant, and the reason for each is written beside it there. Its image is exported on an opaque white page, so the slate scheme shows it as it is, on white.

    Layout Check

    Before it draws anything, the export checks every placed page as drawio draws it, from the SVG drawio exports for that page, and stops on a problem not listed in LAYOUT_EXCEPTIONS in config/pages.py. diagrams.py check runs the same checks without writing images. The rules, in the layout/ package of the scripts:

    • A connector runs in horizontal and vertical segments.
    • Two connectors do not meet a box at one point from different sides. Branches of one trunk, which leave a box together, are allowed.
    • A connector does not pass behind a box that is not one of its ends.
    • A label fits in its box, and loose text crosses no border, covers no picture and sits on no line unless its background masks it.

    The connectors and the place of every label come from the SVG, so a path is checked where drawio routes it. The width of a label is estimated from the advance widths of Inter, and the rules leave a few percent of margin for the kerning the estimate leaves out. A turned label is not checked.

    Pages drawn free hand are listed in FREE_FORM, where only the label rules apply: architecture.png and current-status-developer.png, a hub with spokes and curves, the two OAuth sequence diagrams, whose messages cross the lifelines, and author.png, a flow drawn along a diagonal. The lines of the network pictogram inside Communication are listed in LAYOUT_EXCEPTIONS.

    Design Rules

    Every page follows the rules of docs/assets/brand/README.md, with sizes taken from the width the documentation shows an image at. The restyle step applies them and can be run again on its own output without changing anything.

    • Lines, arrows and borders are primary #003d73 or grey 700 #333b4c. The turquoise and cyan of the palette, and the tints, are only fills, because as a line on white they stay under the three to one contrast floor.
    • A heavy arrow in primary marks the main path of a page: the forward-auth check on the installation pages, the flows between twins and services in federated-dtaas.png, and the platform links in architecture.png and devops.png. Every other arrow is grey 700 at the normal width.
    • Text is Inter, never italic, bold only for a title, and at least 4.5 to 1 on its fill. A fill drawn see-through is replaced by the solid colour it shows.
    • Every label is at least 14 pixels when the image is shown 700 pixels wide, the narrowest content column of the site. A wide diagram therefore needs larger fonts in drawio units than a narrow one.
    • The label of a box is in Title Case, with the short joining words inside a line (of, and, to, with) in lower case. The label of a connector, a UML guard and a note on a sequence diagram state a relation or a condition, so they read as a sentence ("May have", "User login verified"). A URL, a path, a name with an underscore or an inner capital keeps its case. GitLab, WebApp and Node.js have one spelling, and systemd and authcode stay lower case. title_case and sentence_case in design/wording.py hold the rules.
    • A label background only masks a line, in the colour of what lies behind it: the fill of the smallest box holding both ends of the connector, or the page. A label inside its own shape has no background, so it never shows as a white patch on a coloured box. label_ground in design/masks.py holds the rule.
    • One forward-auth box, one corner radius and one line width for every box, one style for every arrow, and one icon per idea:
    Idea Icon
    A person mxgraph.ios7.icons.user
    The web application mxgraph.ios7.icons.globe
    Docker img/lib/mscae/Docker.svg
    GitLab the official logo in the skill's assets/
    A physical or digital twin mxgraph.signs.transportation.car_3 and mxgraph.ios7.icons.home
    A twin that is running mxgraph.signs.sports.running

    A UML diagram keeps its stick figure and a C4 diagram its person, since they belong to the notation. A product logo, such as Traefik or Keycloak, stays its owner's.

    • A note about the picture is page text and not part of the picture.
    • The DTaaS mark sits at the top right corner of the DTaaS boundary, the largest box of the page, which also gets the thicker boundary line. A page that already shows the full lockup as its title gets no second mark. A page in NO_MARK gets neither: its largest box is drawn like every other box.

    Updating a Diagram

    1. Open dtaas-diagrams.drawio in drawio and edit the page.
    2. Apply the rules and shrink any new raster logo. The restyle undoes the hand edits listed in hand-edits.md, so run it on a copy that holds only the new or changed page and bring that page back:
    1
2
3
4
5
6
    python3 .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/diagrams.py restyle \
  docs/assets/diagrams/dtaas-diagrams.drawio \
  docs/assets/diagrams/dtaas-diagrams.drawio
uv run --with-requirements .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/requirements.txt python .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/diagrams.py shrink \
  docs/assets/diagrams/dtaas-diagrams.drawio \
  docs/assets/diagrams/dtaas-diagrams.drawio
    1. Export every page to its place in the documentation:
    1
    python3 .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/diagrams.py export

    The export needs the drawio desktop application. It finds it through DRAWIO, then drawio on the PATH, as on Linux, then its install place on macOS and Windows. On a Linux machine without a display, run the command under xvfb-run -a. On Windows, run python in place of python3. Every image is 1600 pixels wide, twice the widest content column.

    1. Open every exported image and check that nothing overlaps. The scripts set colours, fonts and sizes, and a larger font can need a box moved by hand.

    The export draws text with the fonts installed on the machine. Install Inter first, or drawio falls back to another face without a warning. The diagrams use two weights, 400 and 700, and the documentation site already carries both as woff2 in docs/assets/fonts. fonttools turns each into a font the system can install:

    1
2
3
4
5
    for weight in 400 700; do
  uv run --with-requirements .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/requirements.txt \
    fonttools ttLib.woff2 decompress -o "Inter-$weight.ttf" \
    "docs/assets/fonts/inter-latin-$weight-normal.woff2"
done

    Copy the two files to ~/Library/Fonts on macOS or ~/.local/share/fonts on Linux. On Windows, make the two files in PowerShell, then open each one and choose Install:

    1
    foreach ($weight in 400, 700) { uv run --with-requirements .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/requirements.txt fonttools ttLib.woff2 decompress -o "Inter-$weight.ttf" "docs/assets/fonts/inter-latin-$weight-normal.woff2" }

    How the File Was Made

    The file was converted once from a drawio file that held every installation diagram on a single page, in the drawio default colours. The steps that did it are kept so the conversion can be repeated on a later version of that file:

    1
2
3
4
5
    python3 .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/diagrams.py retheme original.drawio themed.drawio
python3 .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/diagrams.py split themed.drawio split.drawio
python3 .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/diagrams.py restyle split.drawio styled.drawio
uv run --with-requirements .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/requirements.txt python .agents/skills/update-docs-diagrams/scripts/diagrams.py shrink \
  styled.drawio dtaas-diagrams.drawio

    The steps that take files on the command line accept only .drawio files under the current directory.

    retheme maps each colour to a colour of the theme, through the COLOURS table of config/theme.py, which reads the brand colours from client/src/theme/tokens.ts, and replaces the old wordmark with the logo in docs/assets/brand. split gives each diagram its own page. restyle applies the design rules above. shrink scales every embedded raster logo down to the pixels the export draws, turns a JPEG into a PNG with a transparent background, and stores each logo with a palette, which took the file from 3.5 MB to about 750 KB.

    Every edit made by hand after a step, page by page, is recorded in hand-edits.md. A restyle run on a whole page undoes them, which is why the restyle runs on new or changed pages only.
